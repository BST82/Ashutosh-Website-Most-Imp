Pitra Paksha Shraddh 2026 Dates, Importance, and Rituals

Pitra Paksha, also known as Shraddh Paksha or Mahalaya Paksha, is a highly significant period in Hindu tradition dedicated to honoring our ancestors (Pitru). During these days, Hindus perform Tarpan, Pind Daan, and Shraddh rituals to offer gratitude and seek blessings from their forefathers .

By performing Shraddh in Pitra Paksha, one ensures peace to the departed souls and receives prosperity, happiness, and harmony in the family. In 2026, this observance takes place from late September to mid-October.

🗓 Pitra Paksha 2026 Dates

According to the Hindu Panchang, Pitra Paksha in 2026 will begin on Saturday, 26th September and will end on Saturday, 10th October with Sarva Pitru Amavasya .

  • Start Date (Purnima Shraddh): 26th September 2026 (Saturday)
  • End Date (Sarva Pitru Amavasya / Mahalaya): 10th October 2026 (Saturday)

These 16 days are divided according to Tithis where Shraddh rituals are performed for ancestors who departed on that specific lunar day. Below is the complete day-wise calendar.

🙏 Importance of Pitra Paksha

  • It is believed that during this period, the souls of ancestors visit the earth to bless their descendants .
  • Performing Shraddh rituals brings Pitru Kripa (blessings of ancestors) and removes Pitru Dosh.
  • It ensures peace, prosperity, and spiritual upliftment in the family.
  • The southern direction is considered significant during this time, aligning with the ancestors' realm .

🕉 Main Rituals Performed in Pitra Paksha

  • Tarpan: Offering water mixed with sesame seeds and barley to ancestors.
  • Pind Daan: Offering rice balls (Pind) with prayers for peace of the souls.
  • Shraddh Bhoj: Feeding Brahmins, saints, or needy people as an offering to ancestors.
  • Charity (Daan): Donating food, clothes, or other items to the poor in memory of the ancestors .
  • Panchbali: Offering food to creatures like cows, crows, dogs, ants, and deities .

🌸 Sarva Pitru Amavasya 2026 Muhurat & Significance

The last day of Pitra Paksha, known as Sarva Pitru Amavasya or Mahalaya, falls on Saturday, 10th October 2026. On this day, people who do not know the exact tithi of their ancestors can perform Shraddh and Pind Daan for all their forefathers collectively. It marks the end of Pitru Paksha and the beginning of Devi Paksha .

  • Rohina Muhurat: 12:31 PM to 01:18 PM .
  • Aparahna Kaal (Best for Shraddh): 01:18 PM to 03:37 PM .
  • *Amavasya Tithi begins: 09:35 PM on 09 Oct 2026 and ends: 09:19 PM on 10 Oct 2026 .

Note: These timings are indicative. Please verify with your local city Panchang for exact moments.

✅ Conclusion

Pitra Paksha is a divine opportunity to remember and honor our ancestors. By performing Shraddh rituals with devotion, one not only fulfills their duties (Pitru Rina) but also ensures blessings for future generations. Traditionally, it is also a period to refrain from new ventures or celebrations and focus on introspection and gratitude .

May this Pitra Paksha 2026 bring peace to your ancestors and divine grace to your family. 🙏

Navratri Puja 2026 Significance, Rituals & Booking Pandit for Hydrabad

Navratri, meaning "Nine Nights," is a sacred Hindu festival dedicated to Goddess Durga and her nine divine forms. Devotees observe fasts, perform pujas, and seek the goddess's blessings for protection, prosperity, and success. In 2026, four Navratris occur – Chaitra, Magha, Ashad, and Sharad. Below are the correct dates for each.

📅 Navratri 2026 – Dates at a Glance

🌸 Chaitra Navratri

Start: Thursday, 19th March 2026 (Pratipada)

End: Friday, 27th March 2026 (Ram Navami)

Begins the Hindu New Year; marks the victory of good over evil.

❄️ Magha Navratri (Gupta)

Start: Tuesday, 20th January 2026

End: Wednesday, 28th January 2026

A hidden (Gupta) Navratri observed mainly by tantric practitioners.

🌧️ Ashad Navratri (Gupta)

Start: Tuesday, 16th June 2026

End: Wednesday, 24th June 2026

Occurs during the monsoon; also known as Shakambhari Navratri.

🍂 Sharad Navratri

Start: Sunday, 11th October 2026

End: Monday, 19th October 2026 (Maha Navami)

Vijayadashami (Dussehra): Tuesday, 20th October 2026

The most widely celebrated Navratri, culminating in the victory of good over evil.

📌 Note: Exact puja timings (muhurat) vary depending on your city and the specific tithi. For accurate daily timings in Hydrabad or your location, please consult a local Panchang or a qualified priest.

🕉 Step-by-Step Navratri Puja Rituals

  • Ghatasthapana – Setting up the Kalash with sacred water and barley seeds (on the first day).
  • Durga Saptashati Path – Recitation of the 700-verse scripture dedicated to Goddess Durga.
  • Havan – Fire offering for purification and invoking divine blessings.
  • Kanya Pujan – Worshiping nine young girls as symbols of the nine forms of the Goddess.
  • Offering bhog and aarti – Daily offerings and prayers to seek the Goddess's grace.

✨ Benefits of Navratri Puja

  • Brings Goddess Durga’s protection and removes negative energies.
  • Enhances spiritual growth, peace, and devotion.
  • Invites prosperity, success, and harmony in the family.
  • Helps alleviate Pitru Dosh and ancestral afflictions.
